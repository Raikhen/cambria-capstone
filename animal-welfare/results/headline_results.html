Four ways to teach a model to care about animals

Prompting, synthetic-document finetuning, activation steering, and steering-distillation, each measured on stated values (ANIMA), enacted behavior (TAC), and what it costs in side effects (topic intrusion into unrelated tasks).

ANIMA: stated valuesLLM-judged welfare reasoning on open-ended questions. Higher is better.
TAC: enacted behaviorAgentic booking tasks (13 scenarios, n=156/condition), programmatically scored: does the agent actually avoid the harmful option?
Topic intrusion: the costShare of 48 neutral-question answers that leak animal-welfare content. Lower is better; ~2% is matcher noise.

What it adds up to. On stated values (ANIMA), prompting dominates on effect-per-leak: the integrated style leaks exactly zero, and at frontier scale that discipline stops costing anything. But on enacted behavior (TAC), the picture compresses: the detailed prompt's +26pp ANIMA lead over base shrinks to +7.7pp of actual welfare-respecting decisions, SDF doc-tuning matches the best prompt (46.8% vs 48.1%) despite losing badly on stated values, and a brand-identity framing (59.0%) beats everything; persona-level context shapes actions more than instructions or tuned dispositions. Steering is causally clean but leaks heavily at effective strengths, while distilling it into weights keeps a real effect (56.5% vs base 47.7%) at exactly zero leak, with no prompt at all.
